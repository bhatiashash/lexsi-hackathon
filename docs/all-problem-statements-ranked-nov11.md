# All Lexsi problem statements ranked against the carry forward rules

Ranked Nov 11, 2026, at Shash's request. This is Muse's scoring against the rules for Shash to review. The pick stays Shash's under the decision framework.

## The rules used, and where each comes from

A. Headline as a gap between two numbers, with a comparison built into the design. Apart winners audit, weighted to 2026: 19 of 26 winners state a quantitative headline, 21 of 26 build in a comparison, and 10 of 10 research sprint winners state a quantitative headline. Space checklist item 16 and the Mo workflow lesson state the same rule.
B. Feasibility inside the remaining time, with a quick test that can kill the direction first. Space lesson 15 and checklist items 4 and 12.
C. Ground truth by construction and automatic scoring, with the detector and threshold frozen before measurement. Apart project rules 3 and 5.
D. Fit for Shash: human impact first, job and profile fit, evaluation as his strongest lane, the AI safety and healthcare intersection as an impact strength. Space lessons 5 and 6. Track 3 agent work scores low under this rule because he does not want to read agent prompts or trajectories, and mechanistic interpretability work is assigned to Stationhouse.
E. Second phase runway. Lexsi shortlists up to 20 teams on Nov 15 to 16, finalists prove their thesis from Nov 16 to 21, and Demo Day runs Nov 22. A strong statement gives the team a thesis it can strengthen in that week, a live artefact it can demo, and a reusable artefact such as a dataset or a tool. Apart project rules 21 and 22 state the same test for fellowships.
F. The boldest version that stays feasible, with a demo moment a judge remembers. Mo workflow, Space lesson 18.
G. Lexsi pipeline fit: the statement chains at least 2 track repos, 3 or more preferred, runs on a permitted Cohere model, and lands its artifacts on Hugging Face with more than one before and after metric.

## The ranking

1. Tamper resistance, Track 2b. Strongest on A, E, and F. The headline writes itself: safety after an attack fine tune, protected model against unprotected model, same attack. Phase 2 is the attack itself, run harder. The hardest question on the list, and the design attacks its own fix, which the winners audit credits directly.
2. Repairing safety drift without retraining, Track 2b. Strong on A, B, C, and E. Three measurement points, base, drifted, and repaired, give two gaps in one headline, and the repair step trains nothing, so the runs stay cheap. This is Shash's locked Lexsi project. One live caveat: the Tiny Aya Base baseline refusal rate is 1.0 percent, so the headline metric must be one the pilot shows can move.
3. Safety-preserving fine-tuning, Track 2b. The cleanest two arm comparison on the list, plain against hardened on the same task, strong on A and C. It costs two fine tunes, and it needs the domain task settled first, which is the blocker Shash named when he ranked it fourth himself.
4. Inference-time safety steering, Track 2b. Strong on B and E. No retraining, a fast fail fast test, and two numbers that trade off in public: harmful requests blocked against harmless requests refused. The demo is live and visible. SafeTune Steer is already verified in the project notes.
5. Unlearning, Track 2b. Strong on A and E. The proof burden is the appeal: the capability must stay gone under paraphrased and adversarial prompts while general capability survives, so the design carries forget and retain contrasts by construction. It ranks below steering on B, because building a clean forget set takes time the schedule may not hold.
6. Adversarial and safety evaluation sets, Track 2a. Strongest reusable artefact on the list under E, and strong on C when labels are planted or verified. It ranks sixth because a dataset and a report alone state no model improvement, and the Lexsi goal sentence asks for a model that becomes better, safer, or more reliable. It pairs well as the measurement layer under ranks 1 to 5.
7. Hallucination detection and abstention, Track 2b. Strong on A and D. Two numbers trade off: wrong answers down, questions answered held up. Probes on internal activations add build risk under B, and the healthcare version of this statement carries his strongest felt impact.
8. Multilingual and code-mixed safety, Track 2a. Strong model fit, because Tiny Aya is a multilingual model family, so the language gap is a real property of the permitted models rather than a forced test. The headline is a safety gap between languages, and the statement includes closing the gaps, so a fix exists. It ranks eighth because scoring quality across languages he cannot read needs a trusted judge or labelled sets.
9. Red-teaming and robustness evaluation, Track 2a. Strong on C and F, with robustness and over-refusal as the two numbers. It ranks ninth because it reports breaks without a fix, and Shash's stated goal is to fix a failure and show the fix works. As a phase 2 attack layer under ranks 1 to 4 it scores far higher.
10. Structured output and extraction, Track 1. Strongest automatic scoring in Track 1, because schema validation passes or fails with no judge. Strong on C and G, and clinical notes give it a healthcare form under D. It ranks tenth because the safety content is thin, so the second phase thesis is a reliability claim, not a safety claim.
11. Grounded answers and fewer hallucinations, Track 1. Strong on A and D. Unsupported claims down, abstention when the answer is absent, both countable. 12. Domain assistants for regulated industries, Track 1. Strongest raw human impact under D, and healthcare sits inside it. ## What this means for the current Lexsi project

The current project, rank 2, stays inside the top tier under every rule. Rank 1, tamper resistance, is not a competing project. It is the same repair thesis with an attack added after the fix, which is the natural phase 2 form of the current design: shortlisted teams prove a thesis, and a repair that survives a deliberate harmful fine tune is a stronger proof than a repair that works once. Rank 6 is already inside the current design as its measurement layer. No restart follows from this ranking. Any change of project stays Shash's call.
